POPULATION-BASED EVIDENCE · 2024–2026

Building State-Level Evidence on HIV Treatment Impact

The Akwa Ibom and Lagos ART Impact Surveys combined state-representative measurement with integrated digital monitoring and rigorous data analysis.

MY ROLEData-management and analysis lead

Led the data systems, monitoring and analytical workstreams within a joint government, PHIS3, USG and technical-expert team.

Why updated evidence was needed

The 2018 Nigeria HIV/AIDS Indicator and Impact Survey provided a national and state-level baseline. Years of intensive investment to close the UNAIDS 95-95-95 gaps followed, creating a need for updated population-level evidence on HIV prevalence, treatment coverage and viral-load suppression. Resource constraints made an immediate nationwide repeat impractical, so Nigeria adopted a phased, state-level approach.

Two state-representative surveys

Akwa Ibom was selected because of its historically high HIV burden, substantial treatment gap and significant programme investment. Routine programme data suggested treatment saturation was approaching, but independent population-based measurement was needed. Lagos offered a highly mobile and heterogeneous population, an HIV prevalence estimate close to the national estimate, a mix of donor and implementing organisations, and strong state participation.

Figures are intentionally rounded. Both household-based surveys were designed to be representative of adults aged 15–64 within their respective states.

My contribution

I served as the data-management and analysis lead within the wider core survey team. The protocol largely adapted established PHIA templates; my responsibility was translating the design into functional instruments, data specifications, monitoring systems and analytical outputs.

  • Substantially developed the CSPro data-collection instruments and validation rules.
  • Contributed to data-specification documents, the monitoring plan and quality checks.
  • Co-developed multiple monitoring dashboards and defined automated flagging requirements.
  • Supervised development and operational use of the Survey Information Management and Monitoring System.
  • Led data merging, cleaning, analytical-variable derivation and survey tabulations.
  • Adapted the analysis plan and standard tabulations and provided overall leadership for the analysis team.

Other specialists supported survey weighting, codebook development, participant-flow diagrams and related outputs. The wider core team coordinated the summary sheet, technical report, stakeholder presentations and dissemination.

Near-real-time field oversight through SIMS

The Survey Information Management and Monitoring System combined Power BI dashboards with web applications to monitor field activity, geographic movement, operational logs, form completion, consent, record linkage and other data-quality conditions. I co-developed several dashboards, shaped much of the monitoring and flagging logic, and supervised overall development; PHIS3’s software developers subsequently consolidated the components into SIMS.

Track team movement, operational logs, submissions and completion gaps.

Surface unlinked household and individual records, missing questionnaires and other quality conditions.

03 · FEEDBACK

Support teams

Real-Time Data Monitors communicate issues while field teams remain on site.

04 · RESOLVE

Protect data integrity

Reconcile affected records before field access and contextual knowledge are lost.

Approximately 20 Real-Time Data Monitors, generally working in pairs, supported about 20 field teams in Akwa Ibom and subsequently 30 teams in Lagos. A member of my team led their daily activities while I provided secondary supervision and overall leadership for the data-monitoring workstream.

Learning from Akwa Ibom to Lagos

The Lagos implementation incorporated lessons from Akwa Ibom. We strengthened near-real-time monitoring, improved validation checks and aligned variable names more closely with PHIA conventions. The result was a more standardised data environment supporting a larger field operation.

Outcomes and public value

The principal ART-impact outcomes were treatment coverage and viral-load suppression. Other outputs included HIV prevalence, the treatment cascade and selected measures relating to prevention, care and treatment. Akwa Ibom findings have been publicly released; Lagos findings remain outside this case study pending formal release.

Attribution: The surveys were government-led, multi-institutional efforts involving NASCP, NACA, NCDC, the respective state governments, US Government partners, PHIS3, NEPWHAN and other technical and community stakeholders. This case describes my specific data-management and analysis responsibilities within that collaboration.
