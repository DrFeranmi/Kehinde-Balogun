NATIONAL DATA QUALITY · 2021–2026

Getting Nigeria’s HIV Treatment Data Right

How biometrics, verification, audits, analysis and interagency coordination strengthened confidence in national treatment data.

MY ROLEMonitoring and data-use team lead from late 2021

Led dashboards, partner reviews, technical support, DQA analysis, verification monitoring and treatment-number recalibration.

The challenge

Fingerprint capture had been introduced before my involvement, but by late 2021 coverage was progressing too slowly to support effective deduplication. Government and donors intensified expectations while the programme needed a disciplined way to monitor progress, solve implementation problems and use the evidence responsibly.

I was not involved in biometrics activities before late 2021. From that point, I led the monitoring and data-use team tracking progress, supporting implementers and coordinating with health-informatics colleagues.

A multi-pronged response

The work became part of the country’s broader Getting the Data Right initiative. Biometrics was one component within a wider evidence system.

01 · CAPTURE

Strengthen biometric evidence

Performance monitoring, partner support and recapture to distinguish matched from non-matched prints.

02 · VERIFY

Resolve suspicious records

Data triggers flagged duplicate risk; facilities physically verified clients and recorded outcomes.

03 · AUDIT

Check reported treatment data

DQA and folder audits compared NDR reporting with facility folders and EMR records.

04 · RECALIBRATE

Translate evidence into totals

Verified evidence informed recalibration in 2024 and a larger cross-agency exercise in 2026.

My contribution

  • Developed monitoring dashboards and led weekly implementation reviews.
  • Led development of the joint DQA/folder-audit approach; performed substantial interagency analysis, maintained the live dashboard and contributed to reporting.
  • Led NDR-based monitoring for client verification while implementers and facilities performed physical verification.
  • Supported recapture monitoring and technical problem-solving with health-informatics teams.
  • Led my team’s role in the 2024 and July 2026 treatment-number recalibrations.

Technology and decision logic

Fingerprint devices connected to the EMR. Templates travelled to the national database, where an automated biometric identification system using MegaMatcher returned comparison results. The programme combined those results with physical verification and audit evidence rather than treating technology alone as proof of treatment status.

~1.5Mrecords assessed in July 2026
~200Kduplicate records resolved
~200Kdifference in the subsequent aggregate total

The 2026 figures are deliberately aggregate. Operational details and agency identities are withheld because the full exercise is not publicly documented.

Responsible interpretation

The exercise identified data-quality and identity-resolution problems; it should not be presented as proof of fraud. Human factors, capture quality, workflow weaknesses and duplicate representation could all contribute.

I worked across biometrics, DQA/folder audit, client verification, state surveys and recalibration. I was not part of the separate pharmacy and supply-chain data-triangulation work.

Attribution: Getting the Data Right was a national, multi-institutional effort. This case describes my leadership and analytical responsibilities within the wider collaboration; it does not claim sole ownership of institutional methods, documents or outcomes.
